What am I looking at? (and how each tick works)
  • Desired vs observed. Left panel = what you asked for. Right = what's actually running. The reconciler's only job is closing that gap, one step per tick.
  • A tick runs three actors in order: OBSERVER (ingest heartbeats, flip stale hosts to notready, mark dead replicas) → RECONCILER (diff per region; create missing replica rows; bin-pack pending ones onto a ready in-region host with free CPU; drain extras) → AGENT (the stub: advance each replica's phase scheduling→starting→health_check→healthy→active and push a heartbeat).
  • Scale with the +/− steppers — that's the CLI writing desired state. Watch replicas get created and placed.
  • Crash a replica (or click one): it fails, the reconciler notices observed<desired next tick and recreates it — self-heal, nobody asked (scenario 6).
  • Kill a host: it stops heartbeating; after the staleness threshold the observer marks it notready, its replicas are presumed dead, and they reschedule onto other hosts (scenario 7). Revive brings killed hosts back.
  • No capacity: ask for more replicas than the region can hold — extras sit in the pending pool with an alloc_reason, never overcommitted (scenario 9). Free up capacity and they place automatically.
  • This is a teaching toy: state is in-memory JS, not Postgres, and the "agent" is a stub that just flips state — exactly the boundary your real project stubs.
